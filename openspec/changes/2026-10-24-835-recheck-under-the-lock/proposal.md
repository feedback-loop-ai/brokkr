## Why

Every spawn door, a seat's, a sequence step's (`engine.rs:1514-1516`) and each panel member's (`panel.rs:452-453`), rechecks the lease at `admission::recheck` (`dispatch.rs:377`), which reads the reservation without the admission lock (`admission.rs:694-699`). Panel members spawn on scoped threads over one shared lease (`panel.rs:270-291`), so one member's recheck runs beside a sibling's `settlement::spawning`, which seals the same record under the lock by link, rename, then unlink of the prior (`root.rs:408-420`, `376-388`). `standing` (`root.rs:281-298`) opens and stats the record, then looks for its prior: a stat taken between the seal's link and its rename reads two links, and a look taken after the seal's unlink finds no prior, so the read returns none and the door refuses it; `recheck` maps that to `Unknown::Lost`, and the member never spawns: `member driver did not spawn: dispatch refused: … the attempt's reservation no longer stands`. That is the CI signature of both resume tests in #835, verified line by line and reproduced by a scratch stress on this host under load (201 refusals of 26,604 unlocked reads beside 3,000 seals, then 865 of 33,612).

## What Changes

- **The door's read is under the admission lock** (operator's ruling 2, mechanism (a)). `recheck` takes `root::locked` on the coordination root before it enters the run's directory (`reserved_at`) and reads the reservation, exactly as `amend` does for `spawning` moments later (`admission.rs:757-759`), and releases it once the record is read: before the read's failure is mapped to `Unknown::Lost`, before the reach is observed again (`redirected`), before the artifact root's identity is compared, and before the door goes on to `spawning`, which takes its own lock (S1, S2). Every production seal of a reservation holds the lock from its first step to its last sync (S4), so a reader under the lock never observes a seal between its link and its unlink: it reads the record that stood before the seal, the one the seal committed, or the one its undoing put back.
- **The deadline is the one `spawning` takes**: `startup(Instant::now(), Duration::MAX)`, MB3's 30-second startup bound (`broker.rs:100`, `171`), computed inside `recheck` in the effect crate, as `spawning` (`settlement.rs:386`) and `witnessed` (`:336`) compute theirs. `recheck(held)` keeps its signature at the door and in every test that calls it today; the lock-bound variant is `recheck_by(held, deadline)`, after the `witnessed` / `witnessed_by` precedent (`settlement.rs:335-340`), private to admission, whose test is a child module (S1).
- **Each refusal keeps its own variant** (decision 0071 ruling 8). A lock not had by the deadline refuses `Unadmitted::Identity(Unknown::Expired)`; a lock file that is not this user's single regular file refuses `Unknown::Lock`; a run directory that cannot be entered keeps `Unknown::Root`; each as `amend` refuses them today (`root.rs:221-235`). Only the read's own failure maps to `Lost`, as today, which stays the refusal for a reservation that cannot be read or no longer names the attempt standing. No `Unadmitted` text changes. The door's set of refusals is unchanged, for `spawning` already refuses `Expired` and `Lock` at the same door; what moves is their place. The lock is taken first, before the run's directory is entered, the reservation read, the reach observed or the artifact root compared, so a lease refused twice over, by the lock and by one of those four, now reports the lock's refusal, `Expired` or `Lock`, where today the door reports `Root`, `Lost`, `Redirected` or `Replaced` and only the settlement record, after it, would have reported the lock's. In each such case both refuse, nothing is observed and nothing spawns. The delta's second requirement names the four moves, and the door test proves two of them (S2, S3).
- **Every reader of a reservation is audited** (ruling 2), and the audit is written as the delta's third requirement in the form that binds: admission makes no refusal from a read of a reservation taken outside the lock (S4). Every read of `RESERVATION` goes through `root::read` or `root::bytes`, both through `standing` (`root.rs:252-272`, `281`). The production readers and their locks, verified in this tree: `admission::recheck` (`admission.rs:696`; none today, locked by this change); `admission::amend` (`:759`, under `locked` at `:757`; its five callers `settlement::proved` `:317`, `witnessed_by` `:345`, `spawning` `:387`, `settled_driver` `:426` and `admission::release` `:743`); `settlement::standing` (`settlement.rs:553`; its only caller `adopted` locks at `:512`); `inventory/standing.rs:210` through `inventory()` (`inventory.rs:168`), whose three callers lock first: `admit_with` at `admission.rs:623`, `sources::extended_with` at `sources.rs:590`, and `settlement::attestation::attested` at `attestation.rs:43` before `reserved_for` (`:76`). `settlement.rs:309` reads the fold's evidence record, not the reservation. No crate outside the runtime's engine reads a reservation: beyond the broker, `reservation.json` and `RESERVATION` appear in test files only. The implementer confirms each in the commit message, and names the same way any reader this audit missed.
- **Proof, the operator's acceptance** (S5). (i) A deterministic door test binds on the lock: while another thread holds the admission lock, `recheck_by` on a lease whose reservation stands returns admitted only after the release, asserted by order and not by time; refuses `Identity(Expired)` on a spent deadline, for a lease whose reach it would report redirected, unobserved, and for one whose reservation was removed while the lock was held, neither as `Redirected` nor as `Lost`; and refuses `Identity(Lock)` when the lock file carries a second link; each refusal leaves the standing reservation's bytes as they were. ## Decisions

### S1 — The deadline is computed inside `recheck`, and the lock-bound entry is `recheck_by`

`recheck(held)` computes `startup(Instant::now(), Duration::MAX)` and calls `recheck_by(held, deadline)`, after `witnessed` / `witnessed_by`. Handing the deadline in at the door was refused: it would change the door's call and the 22 test callers for no gain, and the door's two lock takes (`recheck`, then `spawning`) are separate waits with the bound each already observes. The crate is an effect crate; reading the clock there is ruling 1's rule, not its exception. `recheck_by` is no wider than its test needs: private to admission, its test in `admission/tests/door.rs`, a descendant.

### S2 — The lock is scoped to the read, not to the door, and the read keeps its place

The lock is taken before `reserved_at` enters the run's directory, as `amend` takes it, and dropped once the record is read, before `redirected`, the identity comparison and `spawning`, which takes its own. U8a2a keeps the host admission lock short-lived: the re-walk and `redirected` observe hands and Git, and holding the lock across them would stall every sibling. Between the two takes a sibling may seal; `spawning` re-reads under its lock and refuses `Lost` only where the attempt is truly gone (H1 of U8e2's first run), as today. The read stays first in the door's order, as `recheck`'s doc (`admission.rs:688-693`) lists it, so the lock is taken before the reach is observed. The consequences of taking the lock first are named, all four: a lease the lock refuses, `Expired` by the deadline or `Lock` by its file, that the door would also refuse as `Root` (a run directory it cannot enter), `Lost` (a reservation gone or no longer naming the attempt), `Redirected` (its reach) or `Replaced` (its artifact root) now reports the lock's refusal where today it reports the other. In every such case both refuse, nothing is observed and nothing spawns; the door's set of refusals and each one's text are unchanged. The delta's second requirement says so per variant, and the door test proves `Expired` over `Redirected` and `Expired` over `Lost`, the refusal the race produced, by the one lock take that orders the other two (S5).

### S3 — A lock not had is not a loss

`Expired`, `Lock` and `Root` are the lock's and the root's own refusals and keep their variants and texts at the door; `Lost` keeps the read-failed and attempt-missing meaning, mapped from the read alone. A reader that cannot tell "the lock was busy" from "the reservation is gone" would hide a fail-open path behind a retry or a fail-closed one behind a wrong name (ruling 8; the judges' high row).

### S4 — The reader rule is the refusal's, not every reader's

The first draft's third requirement read "every read of a reservation holds the lock" and bound "a reader added later" to take it. The clarify judge found its authority wanting: D6 (slice two's `design.md:661-676`) says the admission operation reads and seals under the lock and that the lock "orders admissions only", and the operator's ruling 2 asked for an audit that says of each reader "why it is safe or now locked", which admits a reader that is safe unlocked. The rule is therefore narrowed to the invariant the race broke. `standing`'s read rule (M2, unchanged) finds no record while a sibling's seal stands between its link and its unlink, so a read taken outside the lock can be refused for a seal's transient state; admission makes no refusal of a dispatch, an amendment, an adoption or an attestation from such a read. Today's readers are named with their locks; a reader added later that refuses nothing by its read is not bound to the lock, and one that refuses is. Its authority is the operator's ruling 2 of 2026-10-24, U8a2a's design (every seal of a reservation under the lock) and U8e2's (the settlement record sealed under the lock so a door and an adoption are ordered, H1 of its first run).

### S5 — The lock test binds; the stress is the evidence, sized and exact

The `Hook` seam (`root.rs:86`) hands a seal to its hook at `Staged`, `Committed`, `Retiring`, `Final` and `Undone`, and `commit` (`root.rs:408-420`) calls none between its link and its rename, so the window cannot be held open deterministically without a new `Step`, which changes the seal's steps and is out of scope; nor can the reader's two looks be parted without a seam in `standing`. The binding test therefore holds the lock from another thread (`taken`, `admission/tests.rs:351`, as `an_unprotected_lock_refuses` holds it) and asserts order: the door's thread answers on a channel, the holder receives nothing while it holds, releases, and then receives `Ok(())`. The same test refuses `Expired` on a spent deadline twice over: a lease staked with an unbounded authored reach, which `redirected` reports whenever it is asked, refuses `Expired` and not `Redirected`, the reach unobserved; and a lease whose reservation was removed while the lock was held refuses `Expired` and not `Lost`, so a lock not had is never a loss (S3), and with the lock take removed that lease is refused `Lost` through the held lock, a second assertion the mutation breaks. And it refuses `Lock` on a second link of the lock file, as `an_unprotected_lock_refuses` (`admission/tests.rs:807`) makes one for admission; each refusal leaves the reservation's bytes as they were. A directory, a link or another user's file at the lock's name is refused by the same open and check (`root.rs:222-224`, `238-242`), proven once, at the lock; the door test proves the second link. With the lock take removed the door answers through the held lock, the order assertion fails, and the removed reservation is refused `Lost` instead of `Expired`: that is the binding mutation, recorded in the commit.

The stress, the operator's acceptance: three reader threads each call `recheck_by` 400 times beside one thread that reseals the same reservation through `amend` with an identity amendment, as fast as it can, until a sealing budget of 2 seconds is spent or 3,000 seals are made, whichever is first; all released by one barrier, every take bound to one deadline 20 seconds out and shared by the whole stress. Every thread collects every result, and after the join the test asserts, in this order: each reader's count is exactly 400; every read is exactly `Ok(())`; every seal is exactly `Ok(Commit::Synced)`; the seals made are at least 10 and at most 3,000. Each assertion's message tallies the results that differ, by variant and count, so a read refused `Lost`, `Expired` or `Lock`, a seal late or not proven synced, a reader that read less, and a host that sealed fewer than ten records within the budget each fail the test by name. The mutated stress, the lock take removed, fails on the reads' assertion, and its tally is the count the commit quotes beside the scratch's 201 of 26,604: the clarify judge's third finding, for a reader asserting inline would panic at its first refusal and the run yield at most three.

Why a budgeted phase and not a fixed count, measured on 2026-10-25 on this host (8 cores, load average 17) with the seal's own syscall sequence on a 1.5 KiB record (`.forge/scratch/seal-timing.py` on this worktree): on the worktree's ext4 volume, the one disk-backed place this seat can write, 300 seals took 5.05 s and 5.49 s, a median of 12.7 and 15.5 ms a seal, p95 33.5 and 36.8 ms, worst 43.9 and 49.8 ms; on tmpfs, this seat's TMPDIR, 3,000 seals took 0.131 s, 41 µs each. A fixed 300 seals is therefore 5 to 5.5 s on this disk under load and over 10 s at its p95: within 20 seconds, but by a margin the sweep's other hosts cannot be trusted to keep, and the earlier draft's "300 seals stay within 20 seconds on a slow disk" was the unmeasured premise the judge named. A budget bounds the sealing phase by time, so the deadline can never be spent by sealing: the locked readers, starved while the sealer re-takes the lock at once (`POLL` is 10 ms), wait at most the budget and one seal, about 2.05 s at this host's worst, then finish 1,200 reads of no sync in a fraction of a second, so the whole stress is about 2.5 s on this disk and under half a second on tmpfs against a 20 s bound, and 20 s is under MB3's 30, so no reader waits longer than the door would. The floor of 10 seals in 2 s asks 200 ms a seal, 67 ms a sync: this host's worst observed seal is a quarter of that and its median an eighth, and a host that fails it fails by name rather than passing a stress that was none. The cap of 3,000 is the scratch's count, reached in 0.13 s on tmpfs, so a tmpfs run is bounded too. The readers' 400 each are sized for the mutation's evidence: an unlocked read costs about a third of a tmpfs seal (the scratch's 26,604 reads beside 3,000 seals by three readers), so 1,200 of them fall within the phase's first 150 seals on tmpfs and its first few on disk, under continuous sealing either way, and at the scratch's refusal rates of 0.76% and 2.6% they expect nine refusals or more. The refusal needs a reader descheduled between its stat and its look for the prior, so the mutated stress is load-sensitive evidence, not a proof: the lock test is the proof. `Host::new` makes its HOME in `TMPDIR`, so the implementer runs the stress once with `TMPDIR` on the worktree's disk as well as on tmpfs, and quotes the seal count reached on each in the commit beside the mutation's tally. The council may re-derive the numbers within this rule; every bound stays fail-closed, and the test's comment says why each is where it is and quotes this measurement.

### S6 — Counts, not sleeps

The before and after counts of the two resume tests, alone and in-suite on this host, the stress's refusals with and without the lock take, and the seal counts the stress reached on this host's disk and on tmpfs, are quoted in the report and the commit.

### S8 — `identity` leaves `admission.rs`, confirmed

Triage asked the architect to confirm. `identity` (`admission.rs:776-784`, nine lines; callers `:643`, `:714` and `settlement.rs:523`) observes a directory's device and inode as `root.rs` observes files (`regular_at`, `same`, `single`): it belongs beside them under ruling 10, and it is the helper whose move touches the fewest lines. `sealed` (`:786-796`, eleven lines) is named by `admission`, `settlement`, `attestation` and `sources` in six calls; `settled` and `reserved_at` are shared with `settlement` too, and `redirected` is the door's own logic. The move's consequences, named: `root.rs` gains `identity` and two imports (`MetadataExt`, `Unrooted`); `admission.rs` loses the function and its `MetadataExt` import, and names `identity` in its `super::root` import; `settlement.rs` moves `identity` from its `super::admission` import list to its `super::root` one, two lines, the mechanical consequence of the move and no work of #853, which is how the operator's ruling 1 is read here. Were the council to read ruling 1 as forbidding any line of `settlement.rs`, the fallback is `pub(super) use super::root::identity;` in `admission.rs`, an alias with one consumer, removed when #853 next opens `settlement.rs`; it costs one of the freed lines and gives one item a second name (ruling 5), so it is not the recommendation. No test names `identity` directly (`survey_tests.rs:380` is a closure of its own), so its coverage stays with `admit_with`'s, `recheck`'s and `adopted`'s tests, and nothing else in `root.rs` changes.

### S9 — Where the panel scenario is observed

The first draft's panel scenario ended "both members' records are in the reservation when the attempt ends". A proven attempt is released through `settled` (`admission.rs:767-774`), which moves the attempt from `attempts` to `settled` and its drivers with it, so after release neither record stands. The scenario now names its observation points: the journal, which holds no `member driver did not spawn` for the attempt, and the reservation before the attempt's release, which holds one settlement record per member (`Driver`, `settlement.rs:360-366`), as `efa9af75`'s `effects` print and the restart suite's record assertions read them.
