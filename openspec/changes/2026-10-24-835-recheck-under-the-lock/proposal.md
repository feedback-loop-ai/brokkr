## Why

Every spawn door, a seat's, a sequence step's (`engine.rs:1514-1516`) and each panel member's (`panel.rs:452-453`), rechecks the lease at `admission::recheck` (`dispatch.rs:377`), which reads the reservation without the admission lock (`admission.rs:694-699`). Panel members spawn on scoped threads over one shared lease (`panel.rs:270-291`), so one member's recheck runs beside a sibling's `settlement::spawning`, which seals the same record under the lock by link, rename, then unlink of the prior (`root.rs:408-420`, `376-388`). `standing` (`root.rs:281-298`) opens and stats the record, then looks for its prior: a stat taken between the seal's link and its rename reads two links, and a look taken after the seal's unlink finds no prior, so the read returns none and the door refuses it; `recheck` maps that to `Unknown::Lost`, and the member never spawns: `member driver did not spawn: dispatch refused: … the attempt's reservation no longer stands`. That is the CI signature of both resume tests in #835, verified line by line and reproduced by a scratch stress on this host under load (201 refusals of 26,604 unlocked reads beside 3,000 seals, then 865 of 33,612).

## What Changes

Every production seal of a reservation holds the lock from its first step to its last sync (S4), so a reader under the lock never observes a seal between its link and its unlink: it reads the record that stood before the seal, the one the seal committed, or the one its undoing put back.

## Decisions

### S1 — The deadline is computed inside `recheck`; no `recheck_by` (reversed in part by design D2)

As drafted, `recheck(held)` computed `startup(Instant::now(), Duration::MAX)` and called a private `recheck_by(held, deadline)`, after `witnessed` / `witnessed_by`, so a test could spend the deadline at the door. Design D2 reverses that second half: the deadline is computed inside `recheck`'s own block and there is no second entry, for its only consumer beyond `recheck` was a test whose facts are proven elsewhere (`Expired` at `locked`, with the lock held; the take before the read, by the seal-in-flight test), and its nine lines were what forced S8's move. What stands: handing the deadline in at the door was refused, since it would change the door's call and the 22 test callers for no gain, and the door's two lock takes are separate waits with the bound each already observes; the crate is an effect crate, so reading the clock there is ruling 1's rule, not its exception.

### S2 — The lock is scoped to the read, not to the door, and the read keeps its place

The lock is taken before `reserved_at` enters the run's directory, as `amend` takes it, and dropped once the record is read, before `redirected`, the identity comparison and `spawning`, which takes its own. U8a2a keeps the host admission lock short-lived: the re-walk and `redirected` observe hands and Git, and holding the lock across them would stall every sibling. Between the two takes a sibling may seal; `spawning` re-reads under its lock and refuses `Lost` only where the attempt is truly gone (H1 of U8e2's first run), as today. The read stays first in the door's order, as `recheck`'s doc (`admission.rs:688-693`) lists it, so the lock is taken before the reach is observed. The consequences of taking the lock first are named in S10: a lease the lock refuses, `Expired` by the deadline or `Lock` by its file, that the door would also refuse by any check after the take, the recheck's own, `Root` (a run directory it cannot enter), `Lost` (a reservation gone or no longer naming the attempt), `Redirected` (its reach) or `Replaced` (its artifact root), or the door's after it, the launch record, the charter or the re-walk, now reports the lock's refusal where today it reports the other. In every such case both refuse, nothing is observed and nothing spawns; the door's set of refusals and each one's text are unchanged. The delta's second requirement says so per refusal; the proof is design D4's and D6's: the lock-file test shows the take refuses before anything is read, and the seal-in-flight test shows it precedes the read, the refusal the race produced shown before the fix and gone after.

### S3 — A lock not had is not a loss

`Expired`, `Lock` and `Root` are the lock's and the root's own refusals and keep their variants and texts at the door; `Lost` keeps the read-failed and attempt-missing meaning, mapped from the read alone. A reader that cannot tell "the lock was busy" from "the reservation is gone" would hide a fail-open path behind a retry or a fail-closed one behind a wrong name (ruling 8; the judges' high row).

### S4 — The reader rule is the refusal's, not every reader's

The first draft's third requirement read "every read of a reservation holds the lock" and bound "a reader added later" to take it. The clarify judge found its authority wanting: D6 (slice two's `design.md:661-676`) says the admission operation reads and seals under the lock and that the lock "orders admissions only", and the operator's ruling 2 asked for an audit that says of each reader "why it is safe or now locked", which admits a reader that is safe unlocked. The rule is therefore narrowed to the invariant the race broke. `standing`'s read rule (M2, unchanged) finds no record while a sibling's seal stands between its link and its unlink, so a read taken outside the lock can be refused for a seal's transient state; admission makes no refusal of a dispatch, an amendment, an adoption or an attestation from such a read. Today's readers are named with their locks; a reader added later that refuses nothing by its read is not bound to the lock, and one that refuses is. Its authority is the operator's ruling 2 of 2026-10-24, U8a2a's design (every seal of a reservation under the lock) and U8e2's (the settlement record sealed under the lock so a door and an adoption are ordered, H1 of its first run).

### S5 — The lock test binds; the stress is the evidence, sized and exact (reversed by design D6 and D7)

As drafted, the proof was a door test holding the lock from another thread and asserting order, refusing `Expired` on a spent deadline over `Redirected` and over `Lost` and `Lock` on a second link, beside a budgeted stress (three readers of 400 reads, a 2 s sealing budget, a floor of 10 and a cap of 3,000 seals, one 20 s deadline, per-variant tallies, run on tmpfs and on this host's disk), sized from a seal rate measured on 2026-10-25 (`.forge/scratch/seal-timing.py`; the numbers are in `781b5c30`'s message). Design D6 replaces the door test with two: a seal in flight, modelled by what every seal holds and what a descheduled reader finds, which shows the race's refusal `Lost` before the fix and admission after, deterministically; and a lock file of two links refused `Lock` at the door. Design D7 cuts the stress from the suite: the operator's acceptance is met by the first test's first clause, the stress's mutated form refuses only at a load-dependent rate and proves little when it passes without more machinery, it is a timing test inside the sweep that removes them, and its floor fails by name on a slow host. What stands from this record: the `Hook` seam (`root.rs:86`) has no step between a seal's link and its rename, so the window cannot be held open without a new `Step`, out of scope, nor can the reader's two looks be parted without a seam in `standing`; the lock is held from another thread with `taken` (`admission/tests.rs:351`), as `an_unprotected_lock_refuses` (`:807`) holds it; a directory, a link or another user's file at the lock's name is refused by the same open and check (`root.rs:222-224`, `238-242`), proven once at the lock, and the door test proves the second link. The measurement stays on record and is no longer load-bearing.

### S6 — Counts, not sleeps

No sleep, retry loop or widened timeout is the fix; `recheck` waits on the lock only, to its deadline, then refuses. The before and after counts of the two resume tests, alone and in-suite on this host, the two tests' recorded failures on the unfixed tree, and the scratch stress's counts from `efa9af75`, are quoted in the report and the commit (design D8).

### S7 — No decision document

The change alters no rule. D6's admission operation reads and seals under the lock (`design.md:661-676`); U8a2a added the door's recheck by the operator's ruling of 2026-10-18, and U8e2 put the door's settlement record under the lock; the door's read was the one read of the reservation that could refuse outside it. Mechanism (a) brings it under the lock the others hold, changing no refusal's variant or text, no read rule (M2), no seal step and no lock rule; the third requirement states the invariant the fix restores, with the operator's ruling 2 as its authority (S4). That ruling of 2026-10-24 is recorded here and in the commission. A decision document would record a rule, and there is none new.

Triage asked the architect to confirm which helper leaves `admission.rs` to make room for `recheck_by`; this record chose `identity` (`admission.rs:776-784`; callers `:643`, `:714` and `settlement.rs:523`), moved to `root.rs` with its `MetadataExt` and `Unrooted` imports and a two-line import edit in `settlement.rs`, with a `pub(super) use` alias as the fallback. Design D2 removes `recheck_by`, so the fix is three lines and `admission.rs` ends at 799 within its 800 ceiling (D9): nothing moves, `root.rs` and `settlement.rs` are untouched, and the operator's ruling 1 is honoured by its plainest reading. The move as described here stays the one fallback, only if the gate's own count disagrees with that arithmetic; never a second helper or an alias.

The scenario now names its observation points: the journal, which holds no `member driver did not spawn` for the attempt, and the reservation before the attempt's release, which holds one settlement record per member (`Driver`, `settlement.rs:360-366`), as `efa9af75`'s `effects` print and the restart suite's record assertions read them.

### S10 — Every refusal after the lock take moves behind it; `recheck` keeps its place

The clarify of `781b5c30` found the second requirement's "only four" false. Today the door's one lock take is `spawning`'s, its last check, and the recheck, `verify_record`, the two charter checks and the re-walk all run ahead of it (`dispatch.rs:377-431`); once `recheck` takes the lock first, a lease the lock refuses that any of those would also refuse reports the lock's, whichever of the eight it was, and one whose lock is held to the deadline is refused when the deadline is spent rather than at once. The requirement now says so for every refusal after the take, names today's eight, and names the one check that stays ahead: a spawn composed refused (`spawn.refusal`, `:373-375`), turned away before the door rechecks.

Two ways to keep the old "only four" true were weighed and refused. Moving `recheck` behind the re-walk, so the lock's first take is the door's last check beside `spawning`'s, would keep the dispatch refusals ahead of the lock; but it reorders a door U8a2a set and the operator's ruling of 2026-10-18 placed (`admission.rs:691-692`), reads a charter and re-walks a script directory for a lease that may be gone, puts the reach's observation after the launch record's judgment, and is not the mechanism the operator ruled, which changes how `recheck` reads and not where. Folding the two takes into one, the lock held from the read to the settlement record's seal, would hold the host admission lock across the charter read and the re-walk, which observe files, hands and Git, against U8a2a's short-lived lock (S2). So `recheck` keeps its place, and the requirement tells the truth about it: a lease the lock refuses is turned away at the door's first take, before anything of the spawn is observed, which is the fail-closed order (ruling 8; the judges' high row).
