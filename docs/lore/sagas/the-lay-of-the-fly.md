# The Lay of the Fly

*A saga of this forge — our own, not ancient. Law three of decision 0019:
where a saga retells a real event, it cites its evidence. This one does.*

---

In the young days of the forge, when the machine had first learned to work
on itself, the implement seats delivered two slices, and the work was
handsome. The console gleamed; the panels ran in parallel like oarsmen; the
seats wrote their results and the trail recorded all.

And the operator said: *let it be verified.* For it was the law of the shop
that no work praises itself.

So a second run was kindled, and into it were seated the readers — the
verify seats, whose whole wage is to refute. In the old story it was Loki
who became a fly in the smithy, biting the bellows-hand to make good work
fail. In this shop the fly is *hired*. It is given the diff, the journal,
and the standing instruction: bite.

The fly bit.

In the console's own surface — code an agent had authored, code that had
passed its making — the readers found a stored cross-site scripting flaw:
hostile text that would ride the journal into the operator's browser and
run there. The work was handsome, and it was wrong, in the one way that
matters most: quietly.

No debate was held about feelings. The phase machine read the finding, and
the rule spoke as rules do, without adjectives:

> `REVIEW-RESIDUAL-SECURITY` — review → stop.

The run halted. The flaw never shipped. The remediation was forged in the
next slice, and the residual notes of the readers became a work queue, as
residual notes always do.

---

**What the lay teaches.** Brokkr's fly cost the hammer an inch of handle
because the fly worked *against* the making. Hire the fly, bound by a
wager it can win, and it works *for* the making: the bite lands in review
instead of in the world. A shop that pays its adversary is not cynical; it
is the only shop whose praise means anything.
